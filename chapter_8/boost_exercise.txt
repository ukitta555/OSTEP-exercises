Q5: Given a system with a quantum length of 10 ms in its highest queue, how often
would you have to boost jobs back to the highest priority level (with the -B flag)
in order to guarantee that a single long-running (and potentially-starving) job
gets at least 5% of the CPU?

Assumptions:

Scheduler
1. 3 queues, 10 ms quantum in the top queue. Lower-queue quanta don't matter,
   since job 0 never runs there (see 5).
2. Allotment is 1 quantum per level (-a 1): after a boost, job 0 gets one 10 ms
   quantum at the top, then drops.
3. A boost moves every job to the top queue and resets its allotment. The boost
   itself and context switches take no time.

Workload
4. Job 0 (the potentially-starving job) is CPU-bound, never does I/O, and runs
   much longer than the experiment.
5. Other jobs always keep the higher queues busy (e.g. interactive jobs gaming
   with -S, staggered so one is always ready). Whenever job 0 is below the top
   queue, it gets zero CPU. This is the worst case that "guarantee" points to.
6. After a boost, job 0 may wait behind the others in the top queue, but it
   always gets its full quantum before the next boost.

Measuring "5%"
7. "5% of the CPU" = job 0's CPU time / boost period, in steady state. Ignore the
   startup period before the first boost.
8. "How often" = the longest boost period (fewest boosts) that still gives at
   least 5%, since boosting more often than needed costs throughput.

Answer: 200ms
